Kana vs OpenClaw

November 28, 2026 · YOOANPAPA

OpenClaw is a hugely popular open-source personal agent. Kana is a signed Mac app. Here is what each does better, with sources, and who should pick which.

OpenClaw (formerly Clawdbot) is an open-source personal agent with about 395,000 GitHub stars and a huge community. Kana is a small paid Mac app. They answer the same wish, “handle my chores”, in very different ways. Sources were checked on November 28, 2026.

Where OpenClaw is ahead

  • Reach. It “meets you in the channels you already use” (README). Text it from your phone and your own computer does the work. It got so popular that Kaspersky says it “managed to trigger a Mac mini shortage in several U.S. stores” (Kaspersky).
  • Freedom. It is open source, runs the models you choose, and you can read and change every line.
  • A proven heartbeat. It wakes every 30 minutes by default and follows your checklist (docs). One Reddit user says they “can actually trust the cron system” (Reddit, via Kilo).
  • Free. No license fee.

Where Kana is ahead

  • No setup weekend. One signed DMG. No ports, Docker or terminal. Reddit users quoted by Kilo say they “gave up because it just never ran as it was expected to” and that “every single update ships more bugs” (Kilo).
  • Fewer ways to get hurt. In early 2026, Kaspersky counted over 230 malicious plugins published in about a week and nearly a thousand OpenClaw installations reachable from the internet without authentication (Kaspersky, Feb 10, 2026). Kana opens no network ports, has no third-party skill store, and stops at a card before anything leaves your Mac.
  • Predictable cost. One Reddit user reported spending almost $5,000 at an average of $131 a day (Kilo). Kana is $19 once and uses your Claude plan; her checks stay quiet when nothing has changed.
  • Fast on everyday Mac chores. Measured on the November 28, 2026 release: simple questions in about 1.5–2.5 seconds, 142 Downloads files tidied in about 5 seconds with one card.
  • A face. A hand-drawn character on your desktop shows when she is working and when she is done.

What Kana doesn’t do yet

  • No messenger or phone access. This is OpenClaw’s biggest advantage.
  • Works only while your Mac is awake.
  • Claude only, and Mac only (Apple silicon, macOS 13 or later).
  • Not open source.

Which should you pick?

If you enjoy tinkering, want to text your agent from anywhere, and are ready to secure a self-hosted setup, OpenClaw is hard to beat. If you want something that works the minute you open it, sits on your Mac desktop, and never sends or pays without a press and hold, choose Kana.

FAQ

Is Kana built on OpenClaw?
No. Kana is a separate native Mac app. It thinks with Claude Code running on your Mac and shares no code with OpenClaw.
Can I text Kana from my phone like OpenClaw?
Not yet. You talk to Kana in a speech bubble on your Mac desktop.
Is Kana safer than OpenClaw?
They are built differently. Kana opens no network ports, has no third-party skill marketplace, and asks before every send, payment, booking or deletion. OpenClaw can be configured safely, but its security depends on how you host and configure it.
Which costs less?
OpenClaw is free, but you pay for model usage and hardware. Kana is $19 once and uses your own Claude Pro or Max plan or API key.
